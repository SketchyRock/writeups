## Executive Summary

In my first competitive programming contest, I demonstrated strong foundational problem-solving skills by solving 2 problems independently and contributing strategic guidance to my team's overall placement. Despite limited prior experience, our team placed in the 50th percentile by leveraging a parallel execution strategy where I handled foundation problems, freeing senior teammates to focus on advanced challenges.

**Problems Solved:**
- **Snakey String:** Implemented an iterative solution in Python that correctly parsed input sequences and built the result string through careful state management.
- **Memories of Passport Control:** Translated complex logical requirements into a functional implementation, demonstrating strong problem-comprehension skills.

**Strategic Role:** Took ownership of the problem queue's "Easy" tier, providing clear, debugged solutions that enabled my teammates to focus on harder problems without context-switching overhead.

## Key Learning: Much Room for Mushrooms

This problem consumed the second half of my contest and highlighted both my strengths and knowledge gaps:

**Problem:** Derive a formula for mushroom growth patterns and count visible mushrooms under specific constraints.

**Approach:** Used whiteboarding to sketch test cases and identify logical constraints manually, which successfully uncovered the conditional logic (If X, then Y) needed.

**Bottleneck:** The final counting implementation required parity (even/odd) analysis and combinatorial reasoning. I had the right mathematical intuition but lacked the specific formulation to implement it.

**Takeaway:** Pattern recognition and logical reasoning are strong, but I need deeper practice with combinatorics and discrete math formulas.

## Team Dynamics

**Strategy:** Parallel Execution — I handled foundation-level problems while teammates tackled advanced ones simultaneously.

**Insight:** This strategy is efficient but requires disciplined prioritization and clear communication. Without explicit team leadership on problem queue management, we risked over-investing time in lower-value problems.

## Action Items for Future Competitions

1. **Algorithms & Complexity:** Move from ad-hoc solutions to implementing standard algorithms (DP, graph traversal, number theory) from memory.
2. **Discrete Mathematics:** Build proficiency with combinatorics, parity tricks, and closed-form formula derivation through targeted practice on Kattis/Codeforces.
3. **Team Leadership:** Establish a rotating "queue manager" role to monitor time allocation and suggest pivots when problems stall.
4. **Problem Set Difficulty:** Progress beyond Easy/Medium tiers to build confidence with genuinely hard problems during practice.
