# JMUS Makeathon, Spring 2026

[← All write-ups](../README.md)

We built Jarvis — an AI home automation system that learned your routines and managed your environment through natural language. Smart lights, thermostat, calendar integration, all running off a Raspberry Pi hub. The live demo was genuinely impressive.

We didn't place.

When we asked why, the feedback was: "We loved the presentation, but we're not entirely sure what the product is." We'd optimized so hard for emotional resonance — decision fatigue, better sleep, living instead of managing — that we forgot to clearly answer what it actually did or why it was better than Google Home. The story buried the product.

It's a weird failure mode. You can be too good at presentation and still lose because the audience doesn't understand what they saw.

I'd also floated a different idea mid-hackathon — Frontline, a missing persons locator using peer-to-peer mesh networking and Verizon's disaster-response towers to work offline during natural disasters. We passed on it because Jarvis was nearly done and Frontline would've been hard to finish cleanly. On the last day, another team presented almost exactly that concept and placed higher than us. Their version was less fleshed out than what I'd sketched, but they had a clearer pitch.

## Takeaway

Lead with clarity, add polish second. A mediocre presentation of a clear idea beats a beautiful presentation of a confusing one. Also: don't dismiss your B ideas just because your A idea is already done.
